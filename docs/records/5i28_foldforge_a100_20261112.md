# 5I28 on an A100: FoldForge, nine models (2026-11-12)

The A100 side of the [5I28 benchmark](../guides/BENCHMARK-5I28.md), measured with the same runner (`benchmarks/5i28_ff/`) as the
[H100 record](5i28_foldforge_20261111.md) and not compared with the kits (the kit measurements were made on the H100 only). Data:
[data/5i28_foldforge_a100_20261112.csv](data/5i28_foldforge_a100_20261112.csv) (every cell, both engines, the H100 value and the ratio).
This file is not edited afterwards.

## Conditions

- **Hardware:** NVIDIA A100 80GB PCIe on the cssb3 cluster (nodes `gpu01`-`gpu06`, 4 cards each, shared with other users' jobs: the node's load average
  read after the runs was 2 to 61 on 6 of the 9 jobs' host files, so the nodes were not idle). One GPU per job, one job per node at a time, **8 CPU cores per job** (`--hint=nomultithread`;
  the H100 runs had 16: the 16-core request did not fit the nodes' free threads, and small-n times may be slightly worse for it).
- **Software:** torch 2.13.0+cu129, triton 3.7.1, the same package versions as the H100 environment (compared, not assumed). The environment was
  rebuilt on cssb3 with `pixi install`, which rewrote `pixi.lock` (+21 / -36 lines: `git diff --stat` in `run_a100/git_diff_stat.txt`); the run's
  `meta.json` says `foldforge_dirty: true` for that reason alone, FoldForge `ee301aa`. Jobs call the environment's python directly
  (`tools/activate_env.sh`), because `pixi run` emptied the environment on the compute nodes (driver CUDA 12.8 against `cuda = "12.9"`).
- **Inputs and protocol:** as the H100 run (real MSA and 4 distinct real templates, 4 trunk passes, 200 steps, 5 samples, bf16, compile + CUDA graphs
  `scope: trunk`, 3 warm repeats). The inputs were a copy: the two LMDBs hold the entry `P0113359` only, byte-identical to the originals; the
  converted native checkpoints are the H100 cluster's files.
- **Backend:** `backend: miniworld`. FoldForge's policy makes `cuda` an H100-only backend, so on the A100 every engine class runs its `triton`
  backend or `pytorch`: **these numbers are the Triton path, not the engine's sm_80 CUDA kernels** (the engine has some; FoldForge does not route to them).
- **Engines:** seven models on the PyPI engine 2.5.1. **Chai-1 (all n) and OpenDDE n = 12 on the engine's unreleased `main` at `46857dec3`**,
  which carries the two A100 fixes (a positional-argument `TypeError` in Chai-1's Triton support check; 32-bit offsets in the Triton TriMul output
  projection: an illegal memory access at the OpenDDE structural pair size). On 2.5.1 those cells failed. A control (AF3 n = 1 and 12) on that engine
  gave 1.697 and 41.508 s against 1.699 and 41.605 s on 2.5.1.

## Notes

- **The A100 is 4 to 5.5 times slower than the H100 at n >= 2**, steady across models and sizes (5.2x at 12 and 16 chains). ESMFold2 (3.2-4.0x) and
  Chai-1 (2.2-2.6x) are less slow. Not explained: for ESMFold2 one possible reason is that its atom stack is FoldForge's own PyTorch on both cards,
  but no profile was made on the A100.
- **OpenDDE n = 16 runs out of memory** on the A100, the same allocation as on the H100 (11.26 GiB, one structural pair tensor), in a fresh process.
- **Not checked on the A100:** structures. These are timings; the A100 was not validated against the released structures, and the n >= 12 H100
  atom-block bug (conditioning rows 8448-16832 on the cuda backend) does not apply to the Triton path, as the engine side reports (A100 was fine).
- **Fixed engine unreleased:** the Chai-1 and OpenDDE cells are to be remeasured on the released 2.5.3.
